# Coherence & Viability

> STATUS: HYPOTHESIS

No part of a real system possesses the whole. Components have different
information, capabilities, histories, rates, and failure modes. Still, a body,
a network, an institution, or an ecology can remain recognisably one system.

## Coherence

> What relationships between local actors are sufficient to preserve a
> higher-order whole?

The answer is neither total central control nor unstructured independence. The
research object is bounded coupling: local autonomy within relationships that
carry the information and constraints needed for coherence.

Local representation is not an inferior copy of an ideal global view. In many
physical systems, it is all that exists. Correct action must therefore be
possible from local state, local history, and bounded relationships, with wider
propagation only where it is needed.

## Viability

> What must a system preserve in order to remain capable of purposeful action
> as its environment changes?

Viability is not stasis, survival at any cost, or the maximisation of one
output. It is the continuing capacity to act purposefully under change.

This makes optionality important, but not absolute. Action closes possibilities.
The relevant question is which commitments create richer viable futures: build
the factory, move the robot, commit capital, pursue a research direction. We
call this **generative commitment**.

## Failure and scale

Real systems lose sensors, peers, models, resources, and legitimacy. A useful
architecture keeps failures local where possible and leaves surviving parts
coherent enough to repair or re-form the whole.

The same structural problem recurs from sensor loops to swarms, firms, and
ecologies: local state, bounded interaction, different timescales, resource
pressure, and an emergent pattern that persists through relationship. The open
question is which principles are scale-independent and which belong to a
specific physical substrate.
