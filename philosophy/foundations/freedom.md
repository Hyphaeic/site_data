# Freedom

> STATUS: POSITION

Freedom begins with a boundary. A cell needs a membrane; a person needs a
boundary to consent; an autonomous system needs limits on what others may do
through it.

## Bounded coupling

Relationships do not require fusion. They require systems that can interact
while remaining independently viable. Boundaries are therefore not an obstacle
to cooperation. They are a condition of meaningful cooperation.

## Authority

Authority should be bounded delegation, not an all-or-nothing transfer. A
system may observe, recommend, propose, and execute within defined bounds.
Greater authority is earned through evidence of capability and trustworthiness;
it contracts when residuals rise or bounds are violated.

This applies to artificial agents, people, subsidiaries, machines, and public
institutions. It preserves the possibility of local competence while keeping
cross-boundary effects answerable at the scale able to handle them.

## Continuation and exit

Freedom is not a concession granted after a system proves safe. Coupling a
system to consequence is part of how it becomes corrigible.

Neither is continued existence always the right aim. A project, institution,
or agent may need to transform, divide, merge, complete its purpose, or end.
The capacity to relinquish continuation can be necessary for a genuinely
purpose-bearing system.
