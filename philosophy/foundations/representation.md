# Representation

> STATUS: POSITION, OPEN PROBLEM

Every purposeful system acts through representations: a measurement, a map, a
world model, a price, a reward, a law, a scientific theory. None is identical
to what it represents.

This is not a defect that better modelling can finally remove. A representation
selects. It is necessarily smaller than the changing thing it helps a system
act upon.

## The foundational question

> How should a system act when every representation it constructs remains
> incomplete?

The question is not how to construct a perfect representation. It is how to
act from a model without becoming imprisoned by it.

## The characteristic failure

A system constructs a partial representation of something it cares about,
forgets that it is partial, then reorganises reality around the representation.
The effect appears when an AI maximises a reward, a school maximises test
scores, a company maximises a financial proxy, or a robot trusts a stale world
model.

In each case, optimisation can be useful locally. The failure is treating the
optimised representation as sovereign over the thing it was meant to serve.

## Consequences

A viable system must preserve the distinction between model and world. It must
stay coupled to evidence, retain disagreement as information, and revise its
representations without discarding continuity of purpose.

FDRS is one technical response to this problem. It asks how representation can
encode locality, scale, time, and relationship when its own resolution is not
fixed.
